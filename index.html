Bookkeeping · Monthly Bookkeeping

Stop guessing where your money went.

(Includes a monthly 30-minute review call)

What is bookkeeping — and why does it matter?

  • Bookkeeping is the ongoing process of recording every financial transaction your business makes
  • It keeps your income, expenses, payroll, and tax remittances organized and accurate
  • Without it, you’re running your business on guesses — not numbers
  • Clean books are the foundation for tax filing, financing, and any serious business decision

What’s covered every month

  • Every transaction categorized correctly against your chart of accounts
  • Bank accounts and credit cards reconciled to the penny
  • Payroll tied out and remittances confirmed
  • P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statement delivered
  • GST/HST figures prepared for your next remittance
  • 30-minute review call to walk through the numbers with you

Why closing books every month matters

  • Monthly close means errors are caught within 30 days — not discovered at year-end
  • You always know your true profit, cash position, and outstanding tax obligations
  • Investor updates, bank covenants, and payroll decisions all run on accurate, current data
  • Year-end tax prep takes hours instead of weeks when books are already clean

Documents you’ll need to provide

  • Bank statements for all business accounts (we connect via read-only bank feed where possible)
  • Credit card statements — business cards used for company expenses
  • Payroll reports from your payroll provider
  • Receipts for any expenses not visible on bank or card statements
  • Invoices issued to clients (for revenue reconciliation)
  • Loan or financing statements if applicable

These documents are the raw material for accurate books — and for defending deductions if the CRA ever asks.

Bookkeeping · Catchup & Cleanup

Eighteen months behind? We’ve seen worse.

(Fixed quote after a free ledger review)

The problem: lost or incomplete books

  • Transactions never recorded, or recorded in the wrong accounts
  • Duplicated entries from imported bank feeds nobody reviewed
  • Mixing of personal and business expenses
  • Multiple bookkeepers who each did things differently
  • GST/HST never filed because nobody knew what was owed
  • CRA letters arriving because returns were filed on bad numbers

What we do

  • Free ledger review first — we assess what exists before quoting anything
  • Fixed-price quote before we touch anything, no hourly billing surprises
  • Rebuild the ledger period by period using bank feeds, statements, and available receipts
  • Correct miscategorizations, eliminate duplicates, and remove personal expenses
  • Produce financial statements for each missed period
  • Calculate GST/HST owed for any unfiled periods
  • Hand off clean, current books — then optionally move to monthly bookkeeping

Pricing & timeline

  • Every project is a fixed price — quoted after the free ledger review
  • Price depends on how many periods need work and how complete your records are
  • Most cleanups take 2–6 weeks depending on volume and complexity
  • You don't need to find every old receipt — bank feeds and card statements cover most transactions
  • We'll tell you exactly what we need from you before we start

What you end up with

  • Complete, reconciled books for every missed period
  • Financial statements ready for late tax filing
  • GST/HST figures prepared for CRA remittance
  • A clear picture of what you actually owe — and what you can deduct
  • No more letters from the CRA about missing filings

Bookkeeping · Accounts Payable

Vendor bills paid on time, every time

The problem most businesses have with AP

  • Bills sit in inboxes for weeks — vendors start calling
  • Payments go out without approval, or get missed entirely
  • Duplicate payments happen because nobody tracks what’s been paid
  • GST/HST input tax credits are never captured on vendor bills
  • Cash position is unknown because payables aren’t reconciled
  • Year-end is a scramble to find and code all the old bills

Why it matters

  • Late vendor payments damage relationships and can lose you credit terms
  • Uncaptured ITCs mean you’re paying more GST/HST than you owe
  • Without an aged payables report, you don’t know your true cash obligations
  • Poorly coded AP leads to bad financial statements — and bad decisions

What we handle

  • Every bill entered and coded against your chart of accounts
  • Approval routing set up before any payment goes out
  • Payments scheduled by due date and your current cash position
  • Vendor statements reconciled monthly
  • Aged payables report delivered weekly or on request
  • Duplicate and overpayment detection built into the process
  • HST/GST input tax credits (ITC) captured on every eligible bill — reducing what you owe CRA

FP&A · Financial Modeling

Models that survive a diligence call

What a driver-based model is

A model where every output traces back to a small set of real business drivers — conversion rates, average contract value, headcount, churn. Change an assumption and the whole model updates predictably.

Common model types

  • Fundraise and investor models (Series A/B, SAFE scenarios)
  • Three-statement financial models (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow)
  • Pricing and unit economics analysis
  • Hiring plan and headcount models
  • Acquisition and merger analysis
  • Break-even and runway analysis

Timeline and process

Most models take 1–3 weeks depending on complexity. We start with a scoping call, agree on key drivers and outputs, and review one round of revisions before delivery.

From plan to numbers to decision

What strategic planning looks like with numbers

Most strategic planning lives in slide decks. We anchor it in the financial model — so when the question is “hire two sales reps or one senior engineer,” you can see both options’ impact on runway, EBITDA, and break-even in the same view.

Common planning exercises

  • Fundraise vs. bootstrap: dilution, timeline, and runway comparison

The output

A decision brief: the options, the financial impact of each, the risks that matter, and a recommended path — something you can share with your board without rewriting it.

Tax Planning · Late Tax Filing

Get current, quietly

What we do

Every corporation in Canada must file a T2 annually, even with no income. If you have missed one or several years, the penalties compound and CRA attention increases over time.

What’s included

  • T2 corporate returns for each missed year
  • GST/HST filings where required
  • Penalty and interest review — relief applications where eligible
  • CRA Voluntary Disclosure Program (VDP) where applicable
  • Coordination with CRA on repayment plans if needed

Voluntary Disclosure Program

If you come to CRA before they come to you, and your filings are at least one year late, you may qualify for VDP relief — which can eliminate or significantly reduce interest and penalties.

Tax Planning · Personal Tax Filing

Founder returns are rarely simple

What makes founder returns different

A founder’s personal return intersects with the company’s: salary vs. dividend decisions affect both. An equity event affects your personal capital gains. Multi-province activity creates individual filing obligations.

What we handle

  • T1 personal income tax return (Canada)
  • Provincial returns for multi-province income
  • Salary vs. dividend optimization (coordinated with the T2)
  • Capital gains reporting — equity events, share dispositions
  • T4, T5, T3, and T-slip reconciliation
  • Cross-border income and foreign tax credit (US/Canada)
  • Estimated tax instalments for Q1–Q4
  • RRSP, TFSA, and RESP contribution planning

The salary-dividend question

How you pay yourself out of the corporation is one of the highest-leverage tax decisions you make each year. We run the numbers during the fall planning review and give you a concrete recommendation before year-end.

Cost to File Business Taxes in Canada

T2 Corporate Tax Return — typical ranges

A T2 is required for every Canadian corporation, regardless of whether it had income. These are industry-standard ranges for professional accounting firms in major Canadian cities as of 2025.

Business type Revenue Typical range
Sole corporation, clean booksUnder $500K$1,500 – $3,000
Sole corporation, catch-up requiredUnder $500K$2,000 – $5,000+
Professional corp (physician, lawyer)$500K – $2M$3,000 – $6,000
Operating company, mid-size$1M – $5M$4,000 – $8,000
Holding company (passive income)Varies$2,500 – $5,000
Group of companies (OpCo + HoldCo)Varies$6,000 – $14,000+

These are market ranges, not quotes. Your actual cost depends on your books, prior-year state, and planning scope.

What drives the price up

  • Messy or missing books. If your accountant has to do bookkeeping before filing, expect to pay for both.
  • Multiple fiscal periods. Filing two or three missed years is not just twice the price of one.
  • GST/HST and payroll filings. If these are not already filed, they add cost and CRA coordination.
  • Cross-border or multi-province income. Each adds complexity and filing obligations.

What keeps the price down

  • Books that are closed and reconciled before your accountant touches them
  • Filing on time, every year — catch-up work is always more expensive
  • One entity with straightforward income
  • Working with a firm that does both your books and taxes year-round
